How Might We
// hmw
eliminate designer burnout caused by reactive, development-driven decision-making, without sacrificing continuous delivery quality?
The Problem

Traditional handoff cycles forced designers to respond to developer decisions rather than lead them. Teams were burning out fixing issues in production rather than driving product strategy.

There was no shared design system, so every designer worked in isolation. More than 60% of design time went into bug fixes, leaving almost no room for discovery or innovation. The team had lost the ability to be proactive.

The Solution

Double Track Agile ran discovery and delivery as parallel sprints, formally separating the space where designers could think ahead from the space where they supported delivery. That one structural change ended the reactive loop.

A consolidated Figma design system with tokens, components, and documented variants eliminated inconsistency at the source. Proactive KPI alerts and a democratized data dashboard gave the team visibility to anticipate problems before they reached production.

Key Decision
// design-decision

Double Track Agile. The team was stuck in a reactive loop. By formally separating discovery and delivery tracks, designers stopped being responders and became leaders of the product conversation. It was a structural fix to a cultural problem.

// reflection

It started emerging organically, midway through the project. If we'd planned for it from sprint one, the consistency gains would have compounded faster. The lesson: structural process changes need to be designed as deliberately as the product itself.
