build a scalable, accessible, and consistent design system from zero, in a regulated healthcare environment, without disrupting ongoing product development?
The Problem

There was no design system, no component library, no naming conventions in Figma. Accessibility complaints were surfacing in production, and in healthcare, compliance failure has legal consequences, not just UX ones.

There were no documented design principles. PMs were making product decisions without research backing. The design function existed but lacked the infrastructure to scale or defend its decisions.

The Solution

Built a WCAG/ADA-aligned component library in Figma from zero, prioritizing accessibility-first components over visual refinement. I framed the design system as a risk-reduction tool to get cross-functional buy-in.

Established a design-to-dev handoff process and trained PMs and stakeholders on design system usage. Redesigned the shopping experience flow, which became the flagship proof of adoption: 80% of the product now runs through the system.

Key Decision
// design-decision

I prioritized accessibility-first components over visual refinement. In healthcare, compliance failure has legal consequences. Framing the design system as a risk-reduction tool, not a design preference, is what got buy-in from legal, engineering, and product at the same time.

// reflection

Building from zero is different from inheriting a system. The hardest part isn't the design. It's getting cross-functional buy-in before there's anything visible to show. The first deliverable that mattered wasn't a component either. It was a shared vocabulary.
