How Might We
// hmw
unify the digital presence of a multi-brand telecom giant — acquired from Nextel, Iusacell, and Unefon — into a single, accessible, high-converting experience?
The Problem

Every acquisition brought a different tech stack: PHP, AJAX, Flash, CQ5, Drupal. There was no unified design language, no accessibility roadmap, and no shared component library. Users encountered a different AT&T depending on which URL they landed on.

Accessibility was not on anyone's roadmap until regulatory pressure made it unavoidable. The gap between brand promise and digital reality was enormous — and growing with every acquisition.

The Solution

Led the migration from CQ5 to Adobe Experience Manager (AEM), including a full year of custom component development — a long bet that paid off in long-term governance and content velocity.

Designed the 2021 AEM design system, then led the migration from Magento 1 to Adobe Commerce. Introduced voice contracts for users with disabilities — one of the first telecom accessibility programs of its kind in México. The 2020 AT&T Accessibility Award followed.

Technical Scope

40 Components, One System

The entire system was built for Adobe Experience Manager (AEM) — based on CQ5 (Java) with a database structured around IDs. Required studying directly with Adobe to grasp the development concepts needed to design and deploy the system.

  • Created approximately 40 different components for AEM production
  • CID (Computer Interaction Design) thinking — technical constraints informed before design decisions
  • Atomic Design approach: components built from smaller components, storing information at nodes
  • Templates first, then components — mapping what to embed (sliders, rich text, galleries) before building
  • Extensive documentation: PPTs and PDFs describing templates, components, character limits, and resize properties
  • 2019: failed deployment — Adobe Brazil and Guatemala teams assisted directly; one-year rollback managed
  • 2020–2021: design system became the foundation for all components across the platform

Design System Evolution

Three Challenges That Shaped the System

Building the first design system for AT&T Mexico required overcoming three foundational challenges that no playbook covered:

  • Challenge 1: Finding anyone who had the Digital Guidelines — required navigating from Branding to Compliance, speaking with managers and directors across departments
  • Challenge 2: Establishing communication with US development and design teams — all files were in Photoshop format; had to recreate everything in Sketch, then later migrate to Adobe XD
  • Challenge 3: Creating and demonstrating the system as the only designer, frontend developer, and coordinator on the team

Evolution timeline: Initial Sketch system (2016) → Found Adobe XD files from US agency (late 2018) → Full migration to Adobe XD → Left/Right/Viewports approach (desktop top + mobile right) → Modularity allowed live site changes without downtime (2019–2020).

eCommerce Process · D3

Seguimiento y Atención e-Commerce — 2017

Interactive recreation of the AT&T México internal eCommerce process flow — originally documented in 2017 as the Magento integration went live. Chat agents followed this decision tree to handle order tracking, delivery follow-up, and cancellations. Hover any node.

Key Decision
// design-decision

The implementation required a full year of custom component development — a bet on infrastructure over features. Stakeholders wanted faster visible output. Making the case for foundational investment, and holding the line on it, was the critical design leadership moment.

// reflection

Fifteen years is unusual for a single engagement. Systems that outlast individuals require documentation-first thinking from day one. The components I built in year one were still in production in year twelve — that's only possible when the rationale is written down, not just lived.
